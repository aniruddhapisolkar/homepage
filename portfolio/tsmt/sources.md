## How the pieces fit together

The early chapters build precise language and proof habits. The middle chapters train you to pick a method from the shape of the problem. The applied chapters ask whether the assumptions fit a real situation. The last part asks you to find your own question and decide what evidence could settle it.

Across all five parts, the same test applies. A small example should help you discover a move. A proof should explain why it works. A stretch problem should make you decide how far the idea carries.

## Familiar examples

Some chapters use standard examples from mathematical problem solving. The missing-corner chessboard in Chapter [8](/homepage/portfolio/tsmt/chapter-08/) demonstrates an invariant. The six-person argument in Chapter [9](/homepage/portfolio/tsmt/chapter-09/) is the upper-bound direction of the classical Ramsey result for triples. The prime-producing polynomial in Chapter [31](/homepage/portfolio/tsmt/chapter-31/) is a standard example of a pattern that eventually fails. Their purpose here is to make the choice of reasoning visible.
