Friday - Nov 20 2026

Over the summer break between my junior and senior year, I conducted a language learning experiment. The goal was to see how an extreme stimulus would affect my language learning. The hypothesis was *extreme stimulus -> extreme adaptation*. Specifically, I challenged myself to simultaneously learn Norwegian, Spanish, and French. 

So, I developed a v1 system. There were a lot of problems with it; it was paid, for one, and it also demanded rigorous evaluation on a daily, weekly, and monthly basis. It was honestly really annoying to work with, because I spent more time managing the system than actually learning. It's here in the portfolio section if you'd like to take a look. 

Instead, I developed a v2 system that I'll talk about right here. 

This is much simpler, more flexible, free to execute (I had to pay $15 monthly for the v1 system, the debts of which would accumulate fast over time), and has less external resources to rely on. 

Again, here's my research question + hypothesis, stated more formally. 

> Research Question: Does learning multiple languages simultaneously improve overall language acquisition efficiency compared to sequential learning?
> 
> Hypothesis: Yes, despite some initial interference, learning multiple languages simultaneously will improve pattern recognition ability and overall > language acquisition efficiency, because an extreme cognitive stimulus produces an equally extreme cognitive adaptation.

***

And here's the actual system.

### System (per language)
- **Vocabulary:** 6,000-word core list (self made)
- **Grammar:** concise comprehensive guide (self made)
- **Writing:** short stories in target language + AI correction (Gemini)
- **Speaking:** AI speaking/listening practice (Gemini, don't do this until basic grammar/vocab is internalized)
- **Input:** native reading/listening (variety of sources online)

**Principle**. Move through: **Unknown → Recognized → Retrievable → Usable → Automatic**

Gradually replace **English → target language** translation with **meaning → target language** production.

### Weekly Protocol

**Every Weekday:** 15 min maintenance (do ≥2 of these sessions per language)
- Vocabulary practice
- Grammar review
- Occasional short writing

**Weekend:** 45-min sessions
- Saturday: Spanish + French writing
- Sunday: Norwegian writing + speaking practice (15 min per language; do if condition met)

Target: **~60 min/language/week**, with one rotating extra conversation session.

### Measurement

Track:
- Vocabulary retrieval
- Writing errors
- Speaking performance
- Listening comprehension

Maintain an **observation log** and use it to refine further practice.

Core Loop: **Learn → Retrieve → Produce → Correct → Review → Repeat**

***

I stopped executing this experiment when senior year started because I was busy with school and college applications, but I do plan to pick it back up next summer.
