title: "Engineering mathematical learning"
---

Friday - Nov 20 2026

One of my most ambitious projects has been to see if we can engineer mathematical learning so that if someone wants to learn math, they can learn it fast. 

I have a simple guide on this website called [The Structure of Mathematical Thought](https://aniruddhapisolkar.github.io/homepage/portfolio/tsmt/) that is intended to do just this by teaching the reader the fundamental principles of how to **think** about a mathematical problem, rather than just forcing them to memorize equations.

***

I've experimented on myself to see if I can get extremely good at mathematics, too. I have challenged myself to do something that to me seems crazy.

In freshman year I took Honors Geometry, and in sophomore year I took Honors Algebra 2. By no means are those advanced classes for a 9th/10th grader; those are actually on level classes. I was trying to take Accelerated Precalculus BC (which teaches all of Precalculus in the fall, and then the first 5 units of AP Calculus BC in the spring) in junior year, so that I could take AP Calculus BC my senior year. Problem is, I could only be eligible for that if my Honors Algebra 2 grade was at least a 95%. I was sitting at a 92%.

To be completely honest though, I'm so glad that I was rejected from that program. In junior year I took AP Precalculus, and in senior year right now I'm taking AP Statistics, at high school. But that's not the full story.

In the first semester of junior year, I taught myself all of Precalculus and AP Calculus BC, and smashed the BC exam by getting a 5 on it in May. Now in senior year, I was planning on taking Multivariable Calculus at my school, but yet again I was barred from taking it, as the prerequisite was to take AP Calculus BC *as a class* (I know, it's so dumb). But that turned out to be a blessing in disguise, because that Multivariable Calculus course is 2 semesters long. Instead, I took the 1 semester version at JCCC which is honestly way more rigorous, and next semester of my senior year I'm taking Linear Algebra. I'm also taking Discrete Math 1 this semester, following by Discrete Math 2 the next semester. I'm taking all 4 of these classes at JCCC, which is the sole reason I'm able to accelerate myself in mathematics. 

5 math courses. I'm taking 5 math courses this senior year. If I had not self studied BC in junior year, I would have graduated having done AP Calculus AB, which is just Calculus 1 with a bit of Calc 2 (BC is all of Calc 1 + Calc 2). If I had made a case to take Accelerated Precalculus BC, I would have graduated having done AP Calculus BC. If I had self studied BC and made the case to take Multivariable Calculus, I would not have graduated having taken Linear Algebra & Discrete Math. 

The point is, I feel like mathematical acceleration demands some degree of *extreme delusion* (as does any pursuit, to be honest). You have to be crazy enough to pull off something you're not sure you can do. At the end of 10th grade, I genuinely thought I was just gonna do Calculus 1 and graduate. The reality of my progress is much more astonishing for me; I don't think my 10th grade self could have even comprehended the idea of doing something like this. 

***

So all in all, the prerequisite to engineering mathematical mastery is craziness. Be crazy, and shoot for the moon.
