---
title: "On AI's Value"
---

Wednesday - Nov 25 2026

AI is really good at a lot of things. Like, *really good*. But its distribution of its abilities is really jagged; it's superhuman at some things, subpar at others. I think AI should really only be used for STEM tasks: that is, Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics. Now, that use also means that it should only be *developed* for such tasks. AI models excel at STEM tasks, so why shouldn't we go all into that field? It only makes sense.

I think fields like writing, philosophy, language arts, etc... should be exclusively reserved for humans to excel in. AI models aren't human, after all, so how can they understand the humanities on a deep level? Sure, they can *sound* like they understand it, but it's simply an imitation of the training data that they learned. 

Basically, STEM should be for AI, everything else should be for us.

There's another thing to keep in mind, particularly AI usage by humans. I personally have noticed that I've been using AI more and more to get things done, which is fine as long as those tasks are low-priority. However, it's definitely a slippery slope and I felt like I was using it more and more to do more high-quality tasks. For example, this article: if I hadn't realized what was happening to me, you would be reading the words of an LLM *right now*. 

I didn't want to lose my skills, and my capability. We're making super-intelligence, but that could come at a cost: **our** intelligence. Even if the process of getting things done without using AI is slower than if I did use AI, the benefit is that I won't lose my abilities, and through doing those tasks my ability will actually get better.

There is no shortcut to getting what you want. If you're trying to get things done and they're low-priority, by all means *please* delegate it to AI. But under **no** circumstances should you delegate your highest-priority tasks to it. 

I completely acknowledge that AI is gonna get better and even those high-quality tasks will be doable by it, and I don't have a perfect answer or a strategy that I can give you to navigate that future. But I do know this: don't trade your ability for a sense of accomplishment.
